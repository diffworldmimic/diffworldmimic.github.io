DiffWorldMimic

Scaling Humanoid Loco-Manipulation with Differentiable World Models

Abstract

We present DiffWorldMimic, a model-based reinforcement learning framework for efficient object- and scene-aware humanoid loco-manipulation. Building on SuperMimic, which learns differentiable humanoid dynamics for motion tracking, we extend the world model to jointly predict humanoid and object motion, conditioned on object shape and surrounding scene geometry. By explicitly modeling object translation, orientation, and contact, and incorporating terrain information throughout imagined rollouts, a single differentiable world model captures interactions across diverse objects, scenes, and terrains. This enables efficient learning from demonstrations involving object manipulation and scene-aware locomotion, beyond free-space humanoid motion tracking.

We further apply the same model-based reinforcement learning to a generative controller that follows partial goals from onboard depth. We train a full-reference tracker with model-based reinforcement learning and distill it into a variational student conditioned on partial goals and depth observations. In the same run, the student is also trained with model-based reinforcement learning, with depth maps rendered at the camera poses of the imagined states. In experiments on humanoid-object interactions and scene-aware locomotion, DiffWorldMimic reaches higher tracking success than model-free reinforcement learning with the same observations and rewards in less training time, which shows the effectiveness of shared interaction-aware dynamics and provides a path toward scalable, versatile humanoid loco-manipulation.
